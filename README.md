This MBA (HR Analytics) 3rd semester minor project, titled "Inequality in the Post-Pandemic Indian Economy: A Marxist Analysis of Wealth Concentration", offers a critical exploration into the deepening socio-economic disparities in India following the COVID-19 pandemic. Anchored in Marxist economic theory, the study investigates how the pandemic served as a catalyst for intensifying pre-existing inequalities. It delves into the structural contradictions of capitalism that facilitated wealth accumulation among the elite while marginalizing the working class, particularly informal sector workers.

Using secondary data from sources such as Oxfam, McKinsey, and government publications, the project interprets economic trends through Marxist lenses—especially theories of surplus value, labor exploitation, and capital accumulation. Key focus areas include the impact of state policy on income redistribution, the digital divide, erosion of the middle class, and the rise of monopoly capitalism via corporate giants like Reliance and Adani.

Case studies enhance the empirical rigor, while a strong emphasis is placed on inequality metrics like the Gini coefficient. The project culminates in policy recommendations, including progressive taxation and labor reform, with the aim of constructing a more equitable post-pandemic economy. By blending economic data with ideological critique, the study not only underscores the relevance of Marxist analysis in contemporary India but also contributes actionable insights to HR and economic policy discourse.
